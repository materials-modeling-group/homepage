# Materials Modeling Group
The Materials Modeling Group conducts interdisciplinary research aimed at understanding, predicting, and controlling complex materials phenomena—including deformation, microstructure evolution, corrosion, and fracture—by integrating **physics, mechanics, data science, artificial intelligence (AI), and sensing technologies.**
Traditionally, materials modeling has focused on developing mathematical and computational models to understand and predict materials behavior. Our group extends this paradigm beyond conventional simulation by incorporating experimental measurements, advanced sensing technologies, AI-driven knowledge discovery, and AI systems that collaborate with researchers throughout the research process. Through this integrated approach, we aim to establish **the next generation of research infrastructure for materials science.**
To achieve this goal, we combine physics-based modeling—including multiscale and multiphysics simulations, finite element analysis, and microstructure evolution models—with data-driven methodologies such as surrogate modeling, Physics-Informed Neural Networks (PINNs), and AI agents. By integrating these complementary approaches, we develop new modeling technologies that not only improve the understanding, prediction, and control of materials phenomena, but also transform the way materials research itself is conducted.
Our research includes the development of digital twins for structural materials through advanced crystal plasticity and heat-treatment simulations, high-speed surrogate models and PINNs, AI techniques that automatically extract governing features from microstructural information, and AI agents that automate simulation workflows and support scientific research. We also pursue physics-based studies in finite-element-based metamaterial design, microstructure evolution modeling incorporating microstructural free energy, and the investigation and modeling of corrosion phenomena in extreme environments for heat-resistant alloys and geothermal energy systems.
In addition, we develop innovative technologies for data acquisition and knowledge generation that support next-generation materials research. These include acoustic emission (AE) sensing systems with real-time signal processing, hydrogen visualization techniques and grain-boundary hydrogen diffusion models, and Starrydata, a platform for extracting and structuring graph data from scientific literature.
We view modeling not simply as a computational tool, but as a **common scientific framework for understanding, designing, and advancing both materials phenomena and the research process itself. **
By seamlessly integrating physics-based models, data, AI, and sensing technologies, we seek to establish a new paradigm of modeling science for the AI era and contribute to innovations in materials research and development through the realization of Digital Twins and AI for Materials.
